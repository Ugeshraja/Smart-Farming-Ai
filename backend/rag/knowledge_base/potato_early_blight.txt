DISEASE: Potato Early Blight

CROP: Potato

SCIENTIFIC / COMMON NAME:
Early blight of potato

SYMPTOMS:
Potato early blight commonly produces dark brown to black lesions on older leaves.
Lesions may develop concentric ring patterns, giving them a target-like appearance.
Affected leaves may gradually turn yellow and dry.
Disease symptoms can progress from older foliage toward younger foliage when conditions favor disease development.

FAVORABLE CONDITIONS:
Disease development is favored by warm and humid conditions.
Extended periods of leaf wetness can increase disease development.
Plant stress and poor crop management can increase susceptibility.

MANAGEMENT:
Remove and properly dispose of severely affected plant material.
Maintain good field sanitation.
Avoid unnecessary leaf wetness.
Provide adequate spacing between plants to improve air circulation.
Use healthy planting material.
Rotate crops where appropriate.
Follow locally recommended agricultural disease-management practices.

PREVENTION:
Use disease-free planting material.
Maintain proper plant spacing.
Avoid excessive irrigation that keeps foliage wet for long periods.
Remove infected plant debris after harvest.
Monitor older leaves regularly for early symptoms.

MONITORING:
Inspect older leaves frequently.
Look for dark lesions, concentric rings, yellowing, and drying foliage.
Monitor disease progression after periods of warm and humid weather.

FARMER ACTION:
If symptoms are detected, isolate the affected area when practical, remove severely affected leaves or plants, improve field sanitation and airflow, and consult local agricultural experts for appropriate disease-management recommendations.

IMPORTANT:
Chemical control recommendations should follow the pesticide labels and the recommendations of local agricultural authorities.
